Good interfaces do not compete for attention. They make room for it.

The best tools I use have a kind of **quiet confidence**. They tell me what matters, then get out of the way. No fireworks, no endless badges, no tiny rewards for completing the obvious.

> A calm interface is not an empty interface. It is an interface with priorities.

## Design as editing

Every interface is a collection of decisions about what deserves to be seen first. The work is less about adding the perfect component and more about removing the second-best one.

When a page has a single clear job, its visual language can become specific. A strong headline, a generous margin, and one useful next step are often enough.

## Leave some air

Whitespace is not a lack of content. It is the pause that lets content land. On a small screen, that pause may be the difference between a page that feels generous and one that feels crowded.

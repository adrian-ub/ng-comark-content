We often show work only after it has become certain. That is a shame, because the useful part usually happens earlier.

An unfinished idea still has texture. It shows where the friction is, which assumptions are carrying too much weight, and what the maker is actually curious about.

## A smaller promise

Instead of trying to prove that a direction is correct, make a smaller promise: *this is worth looking at for ten minutes*.

That promise creates room for a better conversation. People can respond to the shape of the idea instead of its polish.

## Keep the door open

The point of a prototype is not to survive unchanged. It is to make the next decision easier.
